1. What is the Internet?

Imagine a vast, invisible web stretching across every corner of the planet. Decades ago, human knowledge was bound by physical walls—libraries, filing cabinets, and localized archives. The birth of the Internet wasn't just a technological upgrade; it was a global shift. It began as a modest whisper between a few interconnected computers, quietly exchanging packets of data. Over time, that quiet connection grew into a continuous, global conversation—linking billions of minds, bridging continents instantly, and forever reshaping how humanity creates, communicates, and learns.

2. The Architecture of Web Evolution

The web did not just grow—it fundamentally transformed in how human intent interacts with digital spaces.

Web 1.0

The Syntactic Web

Read-Only Era (1990–2004)

Characterized by static HTML pages, basic hyperlinking, and centralized content delivery. Users were passive consumers reading digital brochures.

Web 2.0

The Social Web

Read-Write Era (2004–Present)

Driven by user-generated content, interactive web applications, social media platforms, and cloud computing. Users became active creators.

Web 3.0

The Semantic Web

Read-Write-Execute Era (Emerging)

Powered by decentralized networks, machine readability, artificial intelligence, and personalized data ownership. Interactivity becomes contextual and automated.

5. Digital Citizenship & Cyber Safety

Navigating digital spaces requires a commitment to ethical conduct and active personal security.

Core Netiquette Principles

  • Digital Footprint Awareness: Treat every online interaction as a permanent record.
  • Respectful Discourse: Maintain professional tone and empathy across public forums and communication tools.
  • Source Integrity: Verify facts and credit original creators before sharing content to prevent misinformation.

Cyber Safety Protocols

  • Credential Security: Utilize multi-factor authentication (MFA) and unique password hashes across platforms.
  • Phishing Vigilance: Inspect header URLs and avoid suspicious email attachments or unverified site certificates.
  • Data Privacy Controls: Regularly audit social media privacy settings and third-party app permissions.
